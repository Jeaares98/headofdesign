# Salto Führerschein — Growth & Movement OS

> Living document for turning the Salto Führerschein from a course into a recognizable Hamburg movement.

## 0. How to use this document

This is not a strategy deck. It is the operating system for weekly decisions.

Use it in this order:

1. Check the **North Star**.
2. Pick 1–3 priorities from **Now**.
3. Choose content from the **Content Engine**.
4. Turn the best ideas into concrete actions in **Execution**.
5. After publishing or running an activation, record the learning in **Learnings**.
6. Update the ideas, hooks and priorities based on what actually worked.

Rule: if an idea cannot be translated into a concrete next action, it stays in the backlog.

---

# 1. North Star

## Mission

**Wir bringen Hamburg den Backflip bei.**

The Salto Führerschein is the infrastructure behind the mission. The movement should feel bigger than a course.

## Long-term narrative

Hamburg → first 1,000 successful backflips → Berlin → more cities.

## Movement promise

A backflip is not only a trick. It is a visible proof that people can overcome fear, learn something unexpected and complete a real-life sidequest.

## Primary public goal

**1,000 Backflips für Hamburg.**

Every successful first backflip becomes part of a public counter.

Example:

- BACKFLIP #001
- BACKFLIP #128
- BACKFLIP #1,000

The number creates identity, social proof and a reason to follow the story.

## Success metrics

Track only a few metrics:

- first backflips completed
- course bookings / applications
- organic shares and mentions
- creator/community participants
- referral bookings
- local press / media appearances
- repeat community participation

Do not optimize only for views. The important question is: **Did this make more people want to participate, share or talk about the movement?**

# 3. Movement Identity

A movement needs repeatable language and symbols.

## Core language

Use repeatedly:

- **Wir bringen Hamburg den Backflip bei.**
- **1.000 Backflips für Hamburg.**
- **Deine nächste Sidequest.**
- **Sidequest completed.**
- **Backflip #___**
- **Salto bestanden.**
- **Who's next?**
- **Welcome to the Backflip Club.**

Do not invent a new slogan every week. Repetition builds recognition.

## Salto Führerschein as status object

Every successful participant should receive a digital shareable card.

Suggested fields:

- name
- Hamburg
- Backflip number
- date
- Passed ✓
- optional city / location

The asset should be designed to be posted immediately in Stories.

## The Backflip Club

Potential community layer after completing the first backflip.

Entry requirement:

**You completed your first backflip.**

Possible benefits later:

- open training sessions
- community meetups
- advanced challenges
- special drops
- events
- bring-a-friend sessions

Do not overbuild this early. Start as an identity first.

# 4. The Content Engine

Every piece of content should belong to at least one pillar.

## Pillar A — Transformation

Core emotion: **"Wenn diese Person das schafft, kann ich es auch schaffen."**

Formats:

- Before / after
- first backflip reaction
- fear score before vs after
- "vor 60 Minuten konnte sie das noch nicht"
- participant story
- failed attempts → successful attempt

Best recurring format:

1. Person says: "Ich konnte heute Morgen noch keinen Salto."
2. training sequence
3. first successful backflip
4. reaction
5. end card: **BACKFLIP #___**

## Pillar B — Challenge / Entertainment

Core emotion: curiosity + suspense.

Formats:

- "Kann [Name] heute seinen ersten Backflip schaffen?"
- creator challenge
- random Hamburgers learn a backflip
- friend vs friend
- company vs company
- fastest first backflip
- unexpected participant

Best hook structure:

**Can X person complete Y challenge today?**

## Pillar C — Movement

Core emotion: belonging.

Formats:

- counter update
- Hamburg map / neighborhood leaderboard
- milestones
- nominations
- community stories
- "Hamburg lernt Salto"
- city expansion

## Pillar D — Education

Core emotion: competence and trust.

Formats:

- why people are afraid to jump backwards
- common beginner mistakes
- what happens during the first session
- how spotting works
- why small groups matter
- what professional coaching changes

## Pillar E — Founder Journey

Mainly for Personal Brand.

Formats:

- "Ich hatte eine dumme Idee..."
- what we learned from the free pilots
- why 150+ people wanted to participate
- how the course became real
- how pricing works
- why the team currently earns nothing from the course
- working with a nonprofit / club structure
- what went wrong this week
- why Berlin is next
- building the movement in public

## Pillar F — Community Participation

Core emotion: agency.

Formats:

- "Wen sollen wir als Nächstes challengen?"
- nominate a friend
- vote for the next creator
- choose the next city
- choose the next challenge
- vote on merchandise / events / formats

## Pillar G — Conversion

Direct booking content.

Use sparingly but clearly.

Formats:

- next dates
- remaining places
- what is included
- who it is for
- gift / birthday / date / sidequest angle
- clear booking CTA

Rule: most content creates desire; conversion content captures it.

# 5. Viral Loops

The project should not depend only on the founder posting more.

## Loop 1 — Nominate the Next One

After completing a backflip, every participant nominates one person.

Flow:

participant completes backflip → records nomination → tagged person sees it → accepts challenge → becomes new participant → creates next nomination

Template:

**"Ich hab meinen Salto geschafft. Als Nächstes nominiere ich @____."**

## Loop 2 — Backflip Number

first backflip → receives #number → posts certificate → friends see movement → ask / join

## Loop 3 — Creator Chain

creator A learns backflip → nominates creator B → B nominates C → series continues

The creator is not an advertiser. The creator is the episode.

## Loop 4 — Bring a Friend

participant → brings a new participant → both receive a relevant benefit

Potential benefits:

- discounted open training
- priority booking
- community session
- special event access

Keep economics sustainable.

## Loop 5 — Give One Backflip

Partners or sponsors make additional accessible places possible.

Narrative:

**The movement grows and more people get access.**

This can later become a meaningful sponsorship package.

# 8. Personal Brand Framework

## What your account should make people think

Not:

**"Jean-Luc sells a backflip course."**

But:

**"Jean-Luc starts unusual projects, builds them openly with people and turns ideas into real things."**

## Personal Brand Content Ratio

Suggested orientation:

- 40% founder journey / behind the scenes
- 25% human stories / transformations
- 15% community participation
- 10% lessons / insights
- 10% direct promotion

This is a guide, not a rigid rule.

## Founder Story Arc

Use repeatedly:

1. **Idea** — why are we doing this?
2. **Experiment** — can it work?
3. **Proof** — real people succeed.
4. **Problem** — what is difficult?
5. **Build** — how do we solve it?
6. **Community** — people shape the project.
7. **Milestone** — counter / sold out / creator / press.
8. **Next level** — next city / bigger goal.

This creates an ongoing series rather than isolated posts.

## Larger Personal Brand Theme

Potential umbrella idea:

**MAKE LIFE A SIDEQUEST.**

Salto Führerschein = Sidequest #01.

This should only become a larger brand if it naturally resonates. Do not force it too early.

# 9. Idea Prioritization

Every new idea gets scored quickly from 1–5.

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Shareability | Will people naturally send or repost this? |
| Participation | Can people join, vote, nominate or challenge someone? |
| Visual impact | Is the result immediately understandable on video? |
| Local relevance | Does Hamburg have a reason to care? |
| Conversion | Can it create real participants / bookings? |
| Effort | How difficult is it to execute? |

Priority formula:

**Impact = Shareability + Participation + Visual + Local relevance + Conversion - Effort**

Do not spend hours calculating. The score exists to force choices.

- identify top learning
- select 1–3 priorities
- choose weekly hero story

- scripts / hooks
- creator outreach
- participants
- assets
- logistics

## Wednesday — Founder Story

Personal-brand story or build-in-public content.

## Thursday — Community / Education

Poll, nomination, educational post or participant story.

## Friday — Anticipation

Upcoming course / challenge / creator tease.

## Saturday — Capture

Course, live stories, reactions, nominations, BTS.

## Sunday — Proof

Best transformation + counter update + next CTA.

The exact days can change. The important part is the cycle:

**decide → prepare → capture → distribute → learn.**

# 15. Codex Working Instructions

When Codex works on this project, it should treat this file as the source of truth for growth and communication.

## Default Codex behavior

When given a new idea or piece of content:

1. classify it into the correct content pillar or activation type;
2. check whether it supports the North Star;
3. avoid duplicate ideas;
4. propose the simplest executable version;
5. turn it into a concrete next action;
6. suggest the best account: Salto Führerschein, JEAAARES, partner, creator or community;
7. define CTA and success signal;
8. after results are provided, update the Learnings Log and recommend what changes next.

## Example prompt to Codex

> Read `knowledge/salto-fuehrerschein-growth-os.md`. I have this new idea: [IDEA]. Put it into the right part of the system, score its potential, simplify it if needed, and give me the next 3 concrete actions. Do not create unnecessary complexity.

## Weekly prompt to Codex

> Read `knowledge/salto-fuehrerschein-growth-os.md` and `knowledge/salto-weekly-execution.md`. Based on the current backlog, learnings and open actions, recommend the 3 highest-impact priorities for this week. Then update the weekly execution file. Keep the plan realistic and focused on turning ideas into published content, participants or measurable movement growth.
